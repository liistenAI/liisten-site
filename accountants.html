For accountants & bookkeepers · Tranche 2

Client due diligence, drafted from the onboarding meeting.

From 1 July 2026, accounting practices providing certain services — structuring, transactions, acting for clients — are AUSTRAC reporting entities. A careful onboarding conversation already surfaces most of what customer due diligence asks. Liisten records it, with consent, and drafts the record: a detailed file note and an AUSTRAC-mapped preliminary risk assessment, tuned to accounting engagements.

The obligation

Which accounting work is captured.

The regime is service-based, not firm-based: obligations attach when you provide a designated service in the course of business. For accountants, the professional services include:

Transactions for clients

Assisting clients to buy, sell or transfer real estate, entities or legal arrangements, and equity or debt financing.

Creating structures

Assisting clients to create or restructure companies, trusts and other legal arrangements — and selling or transferring shelf companies.

Acting for entities

Acting as — or arranging for someone to act as — a director, secretary, trustee, partner or nominee shareholder.

Holding client money

Receiving, holding or managing client money, accounts or property as part of a designated service, and providing registered-office addresses.

Everyday compliance work on its own — tax returns, BAS, bookkeeping data entry — is generally not a designated service. If your practice provides any of the services above, you enrol with AUSTRAC and run customer due diligence under your own AML/CTF program.

Tuned to your sector

What Liisten listens for in an accounting onboarding.

The analysis is sector-aware: it knows what a new-client meeting should cover, flags what wasn't discussed as open items, and rates only what the conversation actually supports — every rating tied to a verbatim quote and timestamp.

Expected conversation topics

  • Who the client is and the nature of their business or occupation
  • Beneficial owners behind any company, trust or partnership — and why the structure exists
  • Source of funds and source of wealth
  • Cash handling in the business and expected transaction volumes
  • Cross-border links: offshore accounts, entities, suppliers or residency
  • The purpose of the engagement and the services sought

Sector indicators it watches for

  • Structures with no clear commercial rationale
  • Wealth or income inconsistent with the stated business
  • Heavy or unexplained cash intensity; reluctance to use banking channels
  • Unexplained offshore accounts, entities or fund flows
  • Reluctance to identify beneficial owners or explain source of funds
  • Requests that appear designed to obscure ownership or the money trail

Liisten drafts; you decide. The output is a preliminary assessment for your review and signature — not legal or compliance advice, and not identity verification, PEP or sanctions screening. Those run through your existing provider, and your AML/CTF program remains your own.
