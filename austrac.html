AUSTRAC compliance

Your customer due diligence, met in the meeting.

Australia's AML/CTF regime now reaches accountants, lawyers, conveyancers, financial advisers and real‑estate professionals — the "Tranche 2" businesses. Before you provide a designated service, you have to understand a client's money‑laundering risk, and keep a record of how you reached that view. Liisten helps you do that through the conversation you're already having — not another form.

This is a plain‑language guide, not legal advice. Your obligations sit in the Anti‑Money Laundering and Counter‑Terrorism Financing Act and Rules — always work from AUSTRAC's own guidance and your firm's AML/CTF program.

What the rules ask of you

Three obligations, in plain terms

AUSTRAC treats these as related but distinct. Liisten works inside the framework your firm sets, and helps most with the client‑by‑client work in steps 2 and 3.

01

Assess your business's ML/TF risk

Identify and assess the money‑laundering, terrorism‑financing and proliferation‑financing risks your firm faces — across your services, your customers, your delivery channels and the countries you deal with. This assessment underpins your whole program.

Your firm's own document
02

Perform initial CDD before you act

Before providing a designated service, establish on reasonable grounds who your client is and their beneficial owners, whether anyone involved is a politically exposed person, the nature and purpose of the relationship, and — where risk is elevated — their source of funds and wealth. Keep records of how you established each.

Where Liisten helps
03

Assign a customer risk rating

Using the factors from your ML/TF risk assessment, rate each client's risk as low, medium or high — case by case. A low rating lets you apply simplified due diligence; a high rating requires enhanced due diligence. The rating, and your reasoning, must be documented.

Mapped to the framework

Seven factors, aligned to AUSTRAC's categories

AUSTRAC asks you to weigh the kind of customer, the service, how it's delivered and the countries involved. Liisten's preliminary assessment rates each of these, plus the two that most often drive risk — source of funds, and any PEP signal that surfaces in conversation.

Preliminary — by design

Where Liisten stops, and hands off.

Liisten drafts; the practitioner decides. Being clear about the boundary is part of doing this well.

What Liisten does not do

Liisten is a preliminary, evidence‑based assessment — not a formal check. It does not verify identity, and it does not perform PEP or sanctions screening. Its PEP factor only flags what surfaced in the conversation; it is not a screening result. Those formal checks run through your own provider — AML/CTF screening services such as Annature. Liisten also doesn't replace your firm's business‑level ML/TF risk assessment; it works inside it.

What Liisten gives you is the signal for where to look. When the assessment points to elevated risk — a complex structure, undocumented funds, a PEP indicator — that's your cue to apply enhanced CDD and run the formal screening. When it's clearly low‑risk, you have the documented basis to apply simplified CDD with confidence. The signed review is your firm's assessment; Liisten just makes reaching it faster and better evidenced.
