The obligation

What changed for advisers.

If you hold an AFSL and arrange financial products for clients, you're providing a designated service. The 2026 reforms replaced the old simplified program with risk-based obligations:

Arranging is the service

Implementing advice — placing investments, super, platforms and insurance through product issuers and brokers — is item 54, a designated service in its own right.

Initial CDD, risk-based

Identify and verify clients before providing the service, with the depth matched to assessed risk — including the beneficial owners behind companies, trusts and SMSFs.

Enhanced due diligence

Higher-risk clients — PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions, unusual profiles — now require enhanced due diligence. Screening runs through your existing provider.

A risk assessment of your own

Your practice needs its own ML/TF risk assessment and AML/CTF policies — the individual program replaces the one-size-fits-all approach.

Item 54-only providers keep some relief — ongoing customer due diligence remains exempt — but the client-facing work of initial CDD is real, and it starts in the first conversation.

Tuned to your sector

What Liisten listens for in an advice fact-find.

The analysis is sector-aware: it knows what a fact-find should cover, flags what wasn't discussed as open items, and rates only what the conversation actually supports — every rating tied to a verbatim quote and timestamp.

Expected conversation topics

  • The client's circumstances, occupation and objectives, and the products sought
  • Who will hold the investments — individual, joint, company, trust or SMSF — and who controls it
  • Source of funds for contributions, and source of wealth
  • Whether contribution amounts fit the client's income and profile
  • Investment horizon and access expectations
  • Third parties funding, directing or holding authority (e.g. power of attorney)
  • Tax residency, offshore accounts and transfers from abroad
  • Existing super, rollovers and any early-access intentions

Sector indicators it watches for

  • More interest in speed or early exit than in returns; accepting penalties to exit
  • Contributions inconsistent with income, occupation or stated wealth
  • Vague or reluctant answers on source of funds or wealth
  • Third parties funding investments or directing decisions
  • Requests to redeem to unrelated third parties or unfamiliar accounts
  • Investing through structures with no clear rationale
  • Offshore transfers or unexplained foreign tax residency
  • Pressure for early superannuation access

Liisten drafts; you decide. The output is a preliminary assessment for your review and signature — not legal or compliance advice, and not identity verification, PEP or sanctions screening. Those run through your existing provider, and your AML/CTF program remains your own.
