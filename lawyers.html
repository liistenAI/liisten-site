The obligation

Which legal work is captured.

Obligations attach per designated service, not per firm. For legal practices, the captured services include:

Property & entity transactions

Assisting clients to buy, sell or transfer real estate, entities or legal arrangements — including conveyancing — and equity or debt financing.

Creating structures

Assisting clients to create or restructure companies, trusts and other arrangements, and selling or transferring shelf companies.

Corporate & nominee roles

Acting as — or arranging — directors, secretaries, trustees, partners or nominee shareholders, and providing registered-office addresses.

Holding client money

Receiving, holding, controlling or managing client money and property in the course of a designated service — the trust account, in practice.

Litigation, dispute resolution and pure advice are generally outside the regime. If your practice does the transactional work above, you enrol with AUSTRAC and run customer due diligence — identifying clients, the beneficial owners behind structures, and the purpose of the matter — under your own program.

Tuned to your sector

What Liisten listens for in a matter intake.

The analysis is sector-aware: it knows what a intake conversation should cover, flags what wasn't discussed as open items, and rates only what the conversation actually supports — every rating tied to a verbatim quote and timestamp.

Expected conversation topics

  • The matter itself — which designated service is being engaged
  • Who the client is, and whether instructions come from the named client or someone else
  • Beneficial owners behind any company or trust involved — or being created
  • The commercial rationale for any structure being created or restructured
  • Source of funds, and how money will flow — including through the trust account
  • The other parties to the transaction
  • Third parties contributing funds or receiving payments
  • Cross-border elements, and any urgency and its reason

Sector indicators it watches for

  • Money through the trust account with little underlying legal work
  • Funds deposited, the matter aborted, and the refund directed elsewhere
  • Last-minute changes to payer, payee or settlement instructions; overpayments
  • Shell or shelf companies — or several entities at once — with no rationale
  • Nominee arrangements that obscure who controls the entity
  • Instructions from someone other than the named client; evasiveness
  • Unexplained urgency, or indifference to fees or outcome
  • Cash offered for the transaction or fees; unexplained offshore funds

Liisten drafts; you decide. The output is a preliminary assessment for your review and signature — not legal or compliance advice, and not identity verification, PEP or sanctions screening. Those run through your existing provider, and your AML/CTF program remains your own.
