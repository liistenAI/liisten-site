The obligation

What the regime asks of agencies.

If you broker property transactions in the course of business, from 1 July 2026 you're a reporting entity. In practice that means:

Both sides are customers

Customer due diligence applies to sellers and buyers alike — identify and verify them, and the beneficial owners behind any company or trust on the contract.

Know the money

Understand the source of funds for the deposit and settlement — savings, lending, gifts, offshore transfers — with a risk-based approach.

A program of your own

Enrol with AUSTRAC, run an ML/TF risk assessment of your agency, and keep AML/CTF policies that match how you actually operate.

Report what's suspicious

Suspicious matter reports when something doesn't sit right — even if the deal never completes.

Residential leasing (under 30 years) and property management are not designated services — the sales side of the business is where the obligations sit. Developers selling directly are captured too.

Tuned to your sector

What Liisten listens for in a listing or buyer meeting.

The analysis is sector-aware: it knows what a vendor or buyer conversation should cover, flags what wasn't discussed as open items, and rates only what the conversation actually supports — every rating tied to a verbatim quote and timestamp.

Expected conversation topics

  • Which side of the transaction the client is on, and the property involved
  • Whose name the contract will be in — individual, company, trust or nominee — and the beneficial owners behind it
  • The purpose of the purchase or sale, and how it fits the client's circumstances
  • Source of funds for the deposit and settlement
  • Financing: lender or broker involvement and pre-approval
  • Third parties contributing funds or directing the transaction
  • Any cash component
  • Cross-border elements: overseas residency, FIRB position, offshore funds

Sector indicators it watches for

  • Offers to pay any part of the price or deposit in cash
  • Settlement funds from multiple accounts or unrelated offshore entities
  • Third parties funding the deposit where the relationship is vague
  • Buying through a company, trust or nominee with no clear rationale
  • A purchase inconsistent with the client's apparent financial position
  • Willingness to pay well above market without negotiation; sight-unseen buying
  • Rapid resale or flipping patterns; staged or unusual payment structures
  • Requests to redirect deposits to unrelated parties

Liisten drafts; you decide. The output is a preliminary assessment for your review and signature — not legal or compliance advice, and not identity verification, PEP or sanctions screening. Those run through your existing provider, and your AML/CTF program remains your own.
