Security

How Liisten is built and run

Liisten holds recordings of client conversations and the compliance records drawn from them — among the most sensitive material a firm has. This page sets out how that material is protected, in specifics rather than adjectives, and how to reach us if you find a problem.

Last reviewed 29 July 2026 · Liisten — Australian owned, hosted in Sydney.

1. Where your data lives

Everything runs in Amazon Web Services' Sydney region (ap-southeast-2): the application, the database, the file storage, the transcription, and the AI analysis. Meeting content does not leave Australia at any point in the pipeline. The AI model is invoked through an Australian inference endpoint, and the application refuses to start if it is configured any other way — the onshore guarantee is enforced in code, not just in policy.

2. Encryption

  • In transit — HTTPS everywhere, TLS 1.2 minimum with TLS 1.3 supported, on both the web app and the API.
  • At rest — the database is encrypted, as is the storage holding recordings and generated documents.
  • Never public — the database has no route from the internet, and the file storage has public access blocked entirely. Audio is served only through short-lived links that expire in five minutes.

3. Accounts and access

  • Passwords are hashed with scrypt, a memory-hard algorithm chosen to make large-scale cracking expensive. We never store, log or transmit a password in a readable form, and cannot see yours.
  • Sign-in takes the same amount of time whether or not the email exists, so the response can't be used to discover who has an account.
  • Invitation and password-reset links use 256-bit random tokens, stored only as hashes, usable once, and expiring within an hour.
  • Every request is scoped to your firm from your session token — never from anything the browser sends — so one firm's records cannot be requested by another.
  • Roles (owner, compliance officer, team member) govern who can manage people and billing; the server enforces this, not just the interface.
  • Multi-factor authentication (authenticator-app codes, with single-use recovery codes) protects dashboard sign-in — where reports and recordings live. Any practitioner can turn it on, and the firm's owner can require it for every seat. The phone app records and uploads only; its session cannot read reports or administer the firm.

4. The browser application

app.liisten.com.au is served with a strict Content Security Policy, HSTS, and framing denied, so the dashboard cannot be embedded in another site or made to load third-party code. There are no third-party analytics or advertising scripts on the dashboard.

5. Records you can defend

  • A report cannot be downloaded until a practitioner has approved it, and editing an approved report locks it again until it is re-approved — an unreviewed assessment can never leave as a finished document.
  • Every review action is recorded with who did it and when, and each edit increments the assessment's version.
  • Each document states how consent to record was evidenced: captured on the recording itself, or attested by a named practitioner for audio recorded elsewhere.

6. Payments

Card details are handled entirely by our payments provider and never reach Liisten's servers. We store only a reference needed to apply credits to your account.

7. Secrets and change control

Database credentials, signing keys and third-party API keys are held in AWS Secrets Manager and resolved at runtime — they are never stored in configuration files or source code. Production deployments take a database snapshot first, and schema changes are rehearsed against a copy before they are applied.

8. Backups and retention

The database is backed up automatically and protected against accidental deletion. Meeting audio is retained only as long as needed to process and support the record. Signed reports and file notes are firm records, retained securely for at least seven years in line with AML/CTF record-keeping obligations. See the Privacy Policy for how to access or delete your information.

9. Third-party services (subprocessors)

Every third party that touches any data, and what it actually receives. The rule behind the list: meeting content — audio, transcripts, assessments — never leaves Sydney; services outside Australia receive account metadata only (names, emails, billing details, notification text).

  • AWS (Sydney, ap-southeast-2) — all hosting, storage, transcription (Amazon Transcribe) and AI analysis (Anthropic Claude via AWS Bedrock's Sydney profile). The only place meeting content exists.
  • Stripe — payments. Card details go directly to Stripe; we hold references, never card numbers.
  • Resend — transactional email (notifications and receipts). Emails are designed to carry no meeting content.
  • Slack — internal operational alerts (e.g. a new firm signed up). Account metadata only.
  • Google Workspace — our support mailbox.
  • Apple App Store / Google Play — app distribution. No customer data flows to either.

The full register — including data categories and regions per service — is maintained internally, reviewed quarterly, and shared with the security questionnaire on request. New services are assessed against the same rule before anything ships.

10. What we're still building

We would rather tell you what isn't there yet than imply otherwise. Single sign-on (SSO/SAML) and customer-facing audit exports are on the roadmap and not yet available. If either is a requirement for your firm, tell us — it moves them up the list.

11. Reporting a security issue

If you believe you've found a vulnerability, email security@liisten.com.au with enough detail to reproduce it. We'll acknowledge you within two business days and keep you posted while we investigate. Please give us a reasonable opportunity to fix the issue before disclosing it publicly; we won't pursue action against anyone who reports in good faith and doesn't access or alter other people's data while testing.

12. Contact

General security or due-diligence questions: hello@liisten.com.au. We're happy to complete a vendor security questionnaire.
